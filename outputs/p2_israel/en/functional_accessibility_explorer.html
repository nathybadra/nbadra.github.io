FAI methodological weighting architecture
Weights are methodological assumptions used for sensitivity testing and territorial reclassification scenarios. They are not estimated causal parameters. Weights must sum to 1.00 to preserve index comparability and recalculation consistency.

FAI glossary — components, weights and interpretive layers
Composite indices
FAI — Functional Accessibility Indexderived
Composite index evaluating functional residential protection under emergency conditions. Integrates six weighted dimensions: shelter pressure, alert accessibility, miklat coverage, mamad coverage, shelter condition and emergency fallback capacity.
▸ Interpretation & limitations
Interpretation: A high FAI indicates that multiple protective dimensions are simultaneously favourable — adequate supply, low structural stress, accessible within the alert window, good condition and mamad coverage.

Limitations: Sensitivity index, not a predictive model. Does not account for behavioural response, population mobility or real-time conditions.
Nominal security scoremodeled
Pre-FAI residential security index based on structural supply indicators (miklat density, mamad coverage) without incorporating shelter pressure, temporal accessibility or shelter condition.
▸ Interpretation & limitations
Interpretation: Structural baseline. Higher values reflect greater nominal shelter infrastructure per population unit.

Limitations: Overestimates effective protection in high-density or peripheral sub-districts. Does not reflect real-time demand pressure or alert constraints.
Accessibility gapderived
FAI minus nominal security score, expressed in percentage points (pp). Negative values indicate that functional accessibility reduces effective protection relative to the nominal score.
▸ Interpretation & limitations
Interpretation: −15.6 pp means that incorporating shelter pressure, alert windows and condition reduces effective protection by 15.6 percentage points below the nominal score.

Limitations: Sensitive to weight configuration. Interpret alongside scenario results.
Primary components — W1 through W4
Shelter pressure (W1)proxy
Relative modeled stress on the public shelter system. Compares population potentially exposed within the functional walkable radius against estimated miklat / public shelter capacity. Reported as a six-level qualitative signal — Optimal, Low, Moderate, High, Severe and Critical — rather than a raw percentage.
▸ Interpretive levels & limitations
Optimal: low modeled pressure; estimated capacity is sufficient relative to the potentially exposed population.
Low: limited pressure; no immediate signal of structural overload.
Moderate: intermediate pressure; some territorial constraints may appear under alert conditions.
High: elevated pressure; the shelter system may face significant stress during alerts.
Severe: very high pressure; estimated demand may exceed comfortable or safe absorption capacity.
Critical: extreme pressure; the public shelter system may be structurally overloaded relative to exposed population.

Note: This component does not measure physical damage, direct displacement or observed shelter occupation. It is a proxy signal used to identify where protection demand may exceed available shelter capacity.

Limitations: Capacity estimates rely on OSM polygon area conversions. Behavioural distribution across shelters is not modelled.
Alert accessibility (W2)proxy
Share of population able to reach a public shelter within the Pikud HaOref alert window for each territorial unit (15–90 seconds translated to walkable radius).
▸ Interpretation & limitations
Interpretation: Higher values indicate that more residents can reach a miklat within the available warning time.

Limitations: Assumes average walking speed. Does not account for mobility limitations, nighttime conditions or crowding at shelter entrances.
Miklat coverage (W3)proxy
Public miklat availability relative to resident population. Necessary but not sufficient — does not capture distance, pressure or operational condition.
▸ Interpretation & limitations
Interpretation: A high coverage score indicates relatively high per-capita miklat provision, but effective access depends on alert windows and saturation levels.

Limitations: Derived from OSM shelter polygons; coverage quality varies across territorial units.
Mamad coverage (W4)proxy
Proportion of residential buildings constructed after 1993, when private protected rooms (mamad) became mandatory. Reduces dependency on the public miklat network.
▸ Interpretation & limitations
Interpretation: High mamad ratio = large share of residents have in-unit protection, reducing demand on public shelters.

Limitations: Post-1993 construction year used as mamad presence proxy. Does not capture TAMA 38 retrofits or actual mamad operability.
Secondary components & displacement proxy — W5, W6, D9
Shelter condition (W5)proxy
Proxy for operational quality and maintenance of public protective infrastructure. Used because no centralised national inspection endpoint exists.
▸ Interpretation & limitations
Interpretation: Indicative estimate from partial municipal disclosure data. Not a verified inspection score.

Limitations: Primary methodological gap. Pikud HaOref does not publish a consolidated inspection registry. Some municipalities disclose fit / not-fit lists irregularly.
Emergency fallback capacity (W6)proxy
Underground parking and basement-type structures used as de-facto emergency fallback during alerts. Not Pikud HaOref-certified protective infrastructure. Low baseline weight; activated only as a sensitivity scenario component.
▸ Interpretation & limitations
Interpretation: Represents complementary informal capacity, not certified protection. Read alongside the baseline scenario; compare with mamad-dependency scenario to gauge sensitivity.

Limitations: No certification standard exists. Depth and structural quality not assessed. Derived from OSM underground-parking geometry.
Residential displacement pressure proxy (Block D9)modeled
Estimates structural conditions under which households with low functional protection and high urban pressure may experience residential relocation dynamics. Does not measure observed displacement or direct population flows.
▸ Interpretation & limitations
Interpretation: Identifies risk-pressure confluence zones, not flow trajectories. Assembled from CBS, Tax Authority and Knesset RIC data.

Limitations: No direct displacement flow registry exists. Treat as structural pressure signal only.
Methodological framework and interpretive boundaries
This platform does not predict future events or model observed behaviour. It functions as a methodological sensitivity tool: it shows how territorial classifications change when the relative weights of FAI components are modified under different analytical scenarios.
