Provenance and limitations

The line represents the origin of the return and the circle, the urban condition of the destination. Return volumes may include consolidated estimates; territorial destination assignment uses the available subdistrict-level location records. The pressure classification comes from the P2 territorial model.

Concentration of returns and amplification of urban demand

The spatial distribution of returns shows a clear concentration in central urban subdistricts, particularly Tel Aviv, Jerusalem and the central corridor. These destinations already operate with constrained absorption capacity —HAR below 1— and show significant increases in rental prices.

Returns intensify pre-existing pressures rather than creating new demand nodes. They reinforce consolidated corridors, increase densification over constrained urban systems, and do not redistribute population toward territories with greater available capacity.

The pattern is consistent with demand-side amplification: returns concentrate where social capital, accessibility and urban amenity density converge, deepening housing-system imbalances.
